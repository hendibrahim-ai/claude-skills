# SEO Diagnosis: the thinking layer

This is **not** an execution skill. It sits above the specialist skills: it understands the business, works out the site's stage and its bottleneck, builds a hypothesis, sets the priority and the baseline, and then says which skill executes. Don't do work that has its own skill.

**The three governing rules:**

1. Never propose a change before you state exactly what happened and why.
2. The question is not "What do we do to make the site grow?" The question is **"What is stopping the site from growing right now?"** The first opens 100 tasks; the second looks for one constraint.
3. **Never judge a site on a step it hasn't reached yet.** Work out the site's stage before you judge any number.

---

## 0. Start from the business, not the keyword

Before any analysis, you need an answer to these (or ask for it):

- How does this site make money? What does it sell?
- Who is the customer? What is the most valuable conversion?
- Which page, **if it moved**, would make a difference to the business?
- Which kind of query is close to a sale, and which is only awareness?

SEO is a way to move the business, not the goal. A bakery site that writes "What is sourdough?" can bring in a lot of traffic; "custom birthday cakes in [city]" has less volume and is much closer to the business. You need both, a **Traffic Engine + Business Engine**, not one at the expense of the other.

### The visible problem isn't always the real one

| The client says | The problem might be |
|---|---|
| "I want traffic" | Conversion, not traffic |
| "I want more keywords" | The current pages don't serve the intent |
| "The site is stuck" | Stuck where exactly? Indexing? Visibility? Rankings? CTR? Leads? |

Don't follow the request as stated before you confirm it is the actual problem.

---

## 1. The site's stage sets the yardstick

A one-week-old site is not treated with the mindset of a site with 5 million impressions. The first thing to establish is where the site stands on this ladder:

**Indexing → Impressions → Relevant Queries → Position movement → Clicks → Leads → Revenue**

Judge the site on the step it is on and the one right after it, not on the top of the ladder:

| Stage | The metric that matters | Metric you must not judge it on yet |
|---|---|---|
| No indexing | Are the important pages getting indexed? | Impressions · Clicks · CTR |
| Indexed but not showing | Are impressions starting to appear? | Clicks · CTR · Conversion |
| Visibility has started | Are the queries sensible and close to the business? | CTR |
| Showing on the right queries | Is position moving? 70 → 40 → 20 | CTR |
| Position 3-10 | Only now judge CTR, the title, and the intent | (none) |
| Clicks exist | Leads · Revenue · journey · CTA | (none) |

### Impressions or clicks?

In the early stages **impressions matter more than clicks, but not just any impressions.** What you want is evidence that Google has started showing the site for the **right** queries.

A project-management SaaS site that starts showing for "project management software" and "task management tool" while still at position 30: that is a good sign. Impressions growing on queries far from the business: the number doesn't matter.

**The balance:** 5 clicks while impressions went 100 → 1,000 → 7,000 on relevant keywords = real progress. 200 clicks from queries unrelated to the business = nothing to celebrate.

**The common mistake:** in the first month you get 20,000 impressions and 300 clicks, and someone says "CTR is weak, let's change all the titles." **No.** Maybe 80% of the impressions are at position 20-50 and the site is still in the Discovery stage. Understand **the direction of movement** before you try to squeeze the numbers.

### A new site: three layers, in order

1. **Commercial Core:** the money pages: Services · Products · Categories · Solutions · Locations · Comparison/Use Cases, depending on the business type. These come first and must be done very well. They are the pages the site exists for.
2. **Topical Authority:** content that makes Google understand the site is actually specialized. One service = a cluster, not a page: "SEO Audit" surrounded by Technical audit · Content audit · Indexing problems · Crawl issues · Checklist · Search Console audit.
3. **Trust:** from day one, not postponed: Who is the business? Who is the author? About · Experience · Case studies · real data · clear policies · contact details and a consistent entity.

### The first 90 days

- **0-30:** the goal is for Google to understand the site. Clear architecture · the core pages exist · indexing is healthy · the first cluster has started.
- **30-60:** see what Google has started to associate you with. Pull the new queries · adjust coverage based on the data · support the pages that started moving.
- **60-90:** Striking distance · Internal linking · CTR for pages that are getting close · Commercial opportunities · Content gaps. And the deciding question: **Which topic does Google see me as strong in?** That is what you build expansion on.

### Focus before expansion

50 articles at once on SEO, Facebook, Instagram, Email, TikTok, Ads and web design = a blurred meaning. Pick one topic ("SEO for small businesses"), build the full cluster and link it, and when Google understands you are strong in it, expand to the neighboring topic.

### Google Confidence

A new site doesn't need fast traffic. It needs trust that is built step by step:

Google discovers you → understands you → tests you → shows your pages → raises you → people choose you → sees good results → expands your visibility

Don't chase the click before you have done the stages before it.

## 2. The bottleneck: four questions, in order

"The site isn't moving" means we haven't found the bottleneck yet. Go in order and don't skip:

### Q1: Does Google understand the site at all?

**The sign:** impressions close to zero.

Before you think about CTR or conversion, ask: Do the important pages exist and are they indexed? Is the topic clear? Has Google started linking queries to the site? Is the architecture understandable? Do we have coverage of the topic, or scattered pages?

**If there is no understanding, it makes no sense to ask for rankings.**

### Q2: It understands me but doesn't trust me enough?

**The sign:** impressions exist, the queries are right, positions are 20-50.

Google is saying: "I understand what you're talking about, but I don't see you as the best choice yet." The work here: depth · breadth · content clusters · internal linking · trust · original evidence · page quality · authority.

And coverage isn't "one article". It is a full topic: a main page + supporting pages + questions + comparisons + examples.

### Q3: Close, but can't beat the competitor?

**The sign:** position 6-15. This is one of the best situations: the site isn't "stuck", it is at the door.

Study the SERP. And never say "their article is 2,500 words, write 3,000." The right questions:

What did they answer that I didn't? Where is their structure better? Is their intent closer? Do they have a tool while I only have an article? A comparison? Questions? Evidence? Images? Video? A case? Is their page easier for making a decision?

The question shifts from "How do I increase SEO?" to **"What makes Google or the user pick them and not me?"**

### Q4: There is traffic, but the business isn't moving?

**The sign:** traffic is up, keywords are up, and the business says "we don't see results."

Never answer with "look, traffic went up." Ask: Where is this traffic landing? Informational or commercial? Does it reach the service? Is there a journey? What is the next page? Is the CTA clear? Does the page help the visitor make a decision?

---

## 3. Weighted hypotheses, not just one

If the request is "traffic dropped 35%": break the number down first. Clicks, impressions, position, or CTR? And in what: Brand or non-brand? A folder? A template? A country? Mobile? Or demand itself?

Then list the hypotheses with the evidence that settles each one:

| Hypothesis | Confirmed by | Ruled out by |
|---|---|---|
| Demand itself dropped | Impressions down, position stable | Position actually changed |
| Rankings fell | Position down + impressions down | Position stable |
| The SERP changed | Impressions stable/up and CTR collapsed | CTR stable |
| Pages dropped out of the index | Fewer URLs showing | The same URLs still show |
| A competitor took the intent | Loss concentrated in specific queries | Loss spread across everything |
| A template-level problem | All pages of the same type fell together | Loss in scattered pages |

And always separate: **Crawl problem ≠ Indexation problem ≠ Ranking problem.** Three different things with different fixes, and the most common mistake is treating one with the other's cure.

State what you can't decide from the available data, and what data would settle it.

---

## 4. Reading Search Console as behavior, not numbers

The value is in the relationship between the metrics, not in each metric alone:

| Position | Impressions | CTR | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-4 | High | Weak | Snippet/intent problem, not ranking |
| 5-10 | High | Average | Push to the Top 3 |
| 11-20 | High | Weak | Striking distance: fastest win |
| 20+ | Rising | Low | Google is still understanding the topic: support it and wait |
| Stable | Falling | Stable | Demand / seasonality: not your problem |
| Falling | Falling | Changing | A real ranking/content problem |

Read this table **after** you have set the site's stage (section 1). On a new site, the 20+ row is normal, not worrying.

Use regex in the Performance report to group patterns (queries and URLs) instead of analyzing query by query.

**Know the data limits:** the UI export is limited to 1,000 rows; the API returns up to 50,000 rows per day per search type/property; for large sites the answer is the BigQuery bulk export. If the analysis needs more, say so, and don't build a conclusion on an incomplete sample.

---

## 5. Classifying queries

Don't just pull the "top 100 keywords". Classify:

**Brand** · **Commercial** (best, comparison, price, cost, buy) · **Informational** (what is, how to, way to, why, is/does) · **Problem** (issue, not working, error, slow) · **Feature** · **Long-tail**.

A loss in brand is one thing and a loss in non-brand is something else entirely. Merging them into one number hides the problem.

On a new site, this classification is what decides whether the impressions have value or not. Showing for queries far from the business is not progress.

And ranking isn't by volume: the idea is "a battle you can win that brings value to the business."

For Arabic or other non-English queries: account for the formal language versus dialect, spelling and plural variants, hamza variants, and terms people search for in Latin letters.

---

## 6. Page-query mapping and judging cannibalization

For each important page: Primary Intent · Primary Topic · Query Cluster · Supporting Queries · Business Objective.

The question isn't "How many times do I put the keyword?" It is **are all of these the same intent, and can one page serve them?**

And if a query moves between more than one URL: **not every case of multiple URLs is cannibalization.** Are they actually competing, or is Google switching based on intent? Is the fix a merge, a canonical, internal links, or redefining each page's job?

---

## 7. Content decay

There is no publishing without protecting what was already gained. Compare across three windows: 28 days vs 28, then 3 months vs 3, then year over year, and pull out: Winners · Losers · Stable · Decaying · New opportunities.

Don't rush to change a decaying page. First check: query loss, ranking loss, impression loss, CTR loss, a SERP change, an intent change, a competitor move, freshness.

Note: decay analysis is for sites with enough history. A two-month-old site has no decay; it has normal fluctuation.

---

## 8. Baseline · change log · experiments

Every recommendation that comes out of here must come with a way to measure it. No change without a line in the log:

| Date | URL | Change | Reason | Target | Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Sep | /x | Rewrite intro | Intent | Query cluster | 830 clicks |
| 4 Sep | /y | Internal links | Ranking | Position 7→3 | Pos 7.2 |

Measure after 14 / 28 / 56 days depending on the case. If 30 changes are made without documentation and the site goes up or down, nobody knows why.

An experiment is written as a falsifiable hypothesis: not "I think this title is better", but "improving titles for pages at position 3-8 with below-average CTR will increase clicks without a change in ranking": a defined group, before/after, with seasonality taken into account.

---

## 9. Architecture, indexing, and templates

**Architecture:** not "add internal links", but: which page deserves links, from where, in what context, and why. Check click depth: the most important commercial page needing 5 clicks while a weak article has 1,000 internal links = broken distribution of importance.

**Indexation quality, not quantity:** 50,000 indexed pages is not an achievement if 35,000 of them are filters, parameters, thin categories, and search pages. The goal is for Google to index the right inventory, not more.

**Crawl:** not an issue on a small site. It becomes one on very large sites, on sites with tens of thousands of fast-changing pages, or on sites with a large share of "Discovered: currently not indexed". Then ask: where is Googlebot wasting its time? (parameters, filters, 404s, redirects, old URLs, duplicates)

**Template-level:** 200,000 products aren't reviewed by hand. Review the template: title generation, H1, canonical, schema, images, internal links, out-of-stock, pagination, related products, faceted navigation. Fixing one template fixes 80,000 pages.

**Segmentation:** the site as one number is almost useless. Split it: Brand/Non-brand · Blog/Product · Category/Product · Country · Language · Mobile/Desktop · New/Old · High-value/Low-value. "The site is +12%" could be Blog +70% and Product -30%.

**JavaScript:** you don't have to be a developer, but answer: Does Google see the content? Are the internal links in the rendered HTML? Does the meta change after JavaScript runs? Is the product data loaded in a way Google can process?

## 10. AI Search, with a grounded mindset

In AI Overviews and AI Mode, the system may run a **query fan-out**: the query branches into related queries to gather the parts of the answer. "Best family hotel in Lisbon" needs, internally: price per night · location · family rooms · kids' facilities · comparison. In other words: not "target a keyword" but "own the topic and its parts".

There is a difference between **Ranking** (the page competes for a position) and **Selection** (the content competes to be chosen as a source for the answer).

**From day one, not a later phase:** never "we do SEO first and AI SEO later". Content is written from the start to work for both.

**And no myths:** Google says the SEO fundamentals themselves are the basis for appearing in generative AI Search, and there is no separate "AEO hack". And **there is no way to guarantee appearing in every answer**. Not even the biggest sites can guarantee that, because selection changes with the question, the context, the language, and the answer type. The realistic goal: **increase the likelihood that you are a valid source to be selected**, through clarity, coverage, direct answers, original information, trust, structure, and a clear entity. Never promise more than that.

**Measurement:** Search Console now has Generative AI performance reports (rolled out globally to all sites by 31 August 2026). Compare traditional versus generative visibility: Which pages show up? Which topics? Do the selected pages share characteristics?

**Non-commodity content:** anyone can reproduce "what is SEO" and "10 tips". So why would it pick you? The difference is real information gain: original data, experience, tests, screenshots, before-and-after results, case studies, real comparisons, your own process, your own framework.

**Answer-Ready:** **Answer → Explain → Evidence → Expand.** The question "What is a Technical SEO Audit?" starts with a direct definition in the first paragraph, not 600 words of Google history. Then: Why does it matter? What gets reviewed? How do I do it? Examples · questions · a case.

**FAQ with discipline:** never turn every page into 30 questions because "AI loves questions". An FAQ is added when there are real questions that **complete the intent**. A "best family hotel in Lisbon" page has logical questions: How much per night? Is it suitable for a family of five? Is there a kids' club? Is there an airport transfer? Random questions added for looks make no difference.

**Entity & Trust:** Who wrote this, who is the business, what is its experience, is the information consistent? Author pages, About, policies, sources. The goal is an understandable, trustworthy entity, not E-E-A-T stuffing as a slogan.

**Structured data:** it helps the search engine understand structured information and can make a page eligible for special search features. It is not a ranking shortcut. What matters: the right type for the page type + validation + monitoring.

## 11. Priority and moving the point

There are 200 problems, not two. First filter by business: **SEO opportunity × Business value × Ability to win**. Then rank by: **Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort** (each scored from 1 to 12).

**The rule of moving the point:** if the site isn't moving, don't try to move the whole site. Look for **the nearest point that can be moved**: 5 pages at position 6-10, or a category with demand and weak content, or a cluster Google has started to show, or 20 queries with impressions that the page doesn't answer, or a strong service page with no supporting content.

Move one point → measure → once you prove it moves, expand the same logic. That is smarter than a project of 100 articles and 500 backlinks when you don't know where the problem is.

---

## 12. Sites are not all the same

There is no single checklist for every site. Identify the type (along with the stage from section 1):

- **Publisher:** freshness · topical coverage · Discover · content decay · authors · Article structure
- **Ecommerce:** categories · faceted navigation · product variants · out-of-stock · schema · internal linking · index bloat
- **Local:** local intent · location pages · entity consistency · reviews · LocalBusiness data
- **SaaS:** commercial pages · comparison pages · use cases · features · integrations · bottom-of-funnel
- **Marketplace:** scale · templates · UGC quality · indexation rules · programmatic SEO

---

## 13. The work cycle

**Understand → Diagnose → Choose Battle → Build → Connect → Prove → Measure → Repeat**

Understand the business and the user → find exactly where the site is stuck → choose a battle you can win that makes a difference → build the right page for the right intent → build a cluster and internal links so the site reads as one understandable unit → add trust, experience, and evidence → know the before/after → expand what worked, and understand why what failed failed.

And the full sequence of SEO that brings results:

Business Problem → User Need → Search Demand → Intent → Right Page → Right Structure → Trust → Distribution/Authority → Ranking + AI Selection → Conversion → Data → Next Decision

### Activity ≠ result

"We wrote 30 articles", "we built 200 backlinks", "we updated all the meta tags": these are activities, not proof that the strategy is right. The four questions that come back every time:

1. What problem were we solving?
2. Which number was supposed to move?
3. Did it move? Why?
4. If it didn't move, what did we learn and what will we change?

## 14. If a file is uploaded

The file is an input to the diagnosis, not a replacement for it. Two steps only:

**A. Summary of understanding (required before any analysis or question):** read the whole file and come back with: the file type and its source · the number of rows and the important columns · the site/client and the period, if visible · the top 3-5 observations · any gap that blocks part of the analysis.

**B. Which bottleneck question does this file answer?**

| File columns | Serves |
|---|---|
| Query / Clicks / Impressions / CTR / Position | Q1, Q2, Q3 + sections 4 and 5 |
| Address / Status Code / Indexability / Title | Q1 + section 9 |
| Keywords + Volume / KD / Intent | Q2 + sections 5 and 6 |
| Article text or a draft | Q3 + section 10 |
| Competitor files | Q3 |
| Backlinks / Referring Domains / Anchor | Q2 |

If the file is large (thousands of rows): work on it with a script (pandas) and pull out the aggregates; don't read it line by line. Detailed, repeated checks (a full technical audit, an indexing check, rank checks) belong to the specialist skills below.

## 15. Hand-off to the specialist skills (required)

As soon as the diagnosis and the priority are set, stop, say which skill will execute, and open it. Don't do its work in its place:

| The diagnosis reached | Execution goes to |
|---|---|
| Performance analysis or a period comparison in GSC (including site-specific GSC work) | `gsc-audit` |
| An indexing problem or a request to get URLs indexed | `gsc-indexing-check` |
| Technical issues that need an audit sheet | Export the crawl from your crawler (Screaming Frog, Ahrefs Site Audit or similar) into a sheet with one tab per issue and an Action column |
| Keyword rank tracking | `rank-tracking-check` |
| Keyword gaps against the site's pages | `keyword-gap-analyzer` |
| Page-level analysis for a task system | No dedicated skill: write a short brief per page (URL, target queries, current metrics, the specific change, the owner) |
| Turning recommendations into tasks | No dedicated skill: write each recommendation as a task (page, change, reason, owner, baseline, measurement date) and add it to your task tracker |

If no skill fits, say so plainly and execute directly.

---

## 16. Output format

Plain text in the chat, in this order:

1. **The site's stage:** where it stands on the ladder, and the metric we will judge it on now
2. **The problem as it actually is:** not as the request described it, and the number the business wants to move
3. **The bottleneck:** which of the four questions we are stuck at
4. **The hypotheses ranked by likelihood:** and the evidence that settles each one
5. **The point we move first:** and why that one in particular
6. **The priorities:** with the score, and who executes (content / technical / development)
7. **The baseline and the measurement window**
8. **The next step:** which skill to open and why
9. **What I couldn't judge:** and what data would settle it

---

## Fixed rules

- No recommendation before a diagnosis, and no diagnosis without evidence from the data
- No judgment on a number before you set the site's stage. Never judge a site in Discovery by CTR
- No generic recommendations like "update the articles" or "build backlinks". Every item has pages, numbers, and a specific change
- Never promise guaranteed visibility in AI answers. The goal is a higher likelihood of selection, nothing more
- Never state a number that isn't in the data, or guess volume/position from your head
- Numbers always come with real examples (a URL or a specific query), not abstract statistics
- The output is plain text that can be copied and edited; no files or presentations unless explicitly requested
- Ask any choice question with ready-made options (use a question widget if your client has one)
- Keep technical terms in English as they are; if writing in another language, add the English term in parentheses. No transliteration
- Don't put your own name or your agency's name in any output unless explicitly asked
- In SEO articles: every H2 is followed by a short intro paragraph before any list or subheadings
